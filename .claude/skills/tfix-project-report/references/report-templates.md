# Report templates

Use the matching structure. Keep headings as written so readers (banks, KSUM evaluators, investors)
find what they expect. Every template ends with the same five closing sections (see bottom).

---

## DPR — Detailed Project Report for a bank / MSME loan

1. **Cover** — project name, applicant entity, address, contact, loan applied for, date.
2. **Executive summary** — what, where, cost, funding, key ratios (DSCR avg/min, break-even), ask.
3. **Promoters** — names, roles, qualifications, experience (as supplied), shareholding.
4. **Business description** — services (print, repair, …), customers (CUSAT students etc.),
   location advantage, current operations and track record (with real numbers if available).
5. **Market and demand** — sourced figures only (see `india-facts.md`), local demand evidence
   (student population near the shop — cite source), competition, pricing (shop rates).
6. **Technical/operations** — equipment list with makes/models and quotes, process flow
   (online order → shop server → print/repair → pickup), capacity (pages/hour, repairs/day),
   utilities, staffing.
7. **Project cost** — table: land/building (if any), plant & machinery, IT/software, furniture &
   fit-out, pre-operative, contingency, working capital margin. Source per line (quote/estimate).
8. **Means of finance** — promoter contribution, term loan, WC loan, grants/subsidy, equity.
   Scheme fit (MUDRA category / CGTMSE cover) with eligibility check.
9. **Financial projections** (from `scripts/projections.py`) — assumptions table, projected P&L,
   cash flow, balance sheet, loan repayment schedule, DSCR, break-even, sensitivity (−10%/−20%
   revenue, +10% costs).
10. **SWOT and risk mitigation.**
11. **Statutory approvals and compliance** status table (from Legal lens).
12. **Social and environmental impact** — jobs, green counter (paper saved), e-waste handling.
13. **Conclusion and request.**

## Grant / scheme application (KSUM, SISFS, DPIIT-linked)

Mirror the scheme's own form when available (fetch it). Otherwise:
1. Problem and solution (one page, evidence-backed).
2. Innovation — what is new (Smart print check, Phone Doctor, tamper-evident rewards ledger,
   one-ID platform) and why it is technology-led. Be honest: describe rule-based features as such.
3. Current stage (idea / prototype / MVP / revenue) with proof (live URLs, screenshots, numbers).
4. Market and beneficiaries (students; sourced sizes).
5. Team.
6. Milestones and budget — table of milestones with deliverable, month, amount, and how
   completion will be shown (the grant pays in milestone tranches).
7. Eligibility checklist against the scheme rules (with source and date checked).
8. Impact — jobs, students served, paper saved, local partners.
9. Documents checklist (incorporation, DPIIT, KSUM Unique ID, pitch deck, bank details…).

## Investor report / memo

1. One-line pitch and the ask (amount, instrument, use).
2. Problem → solution → why now.
3. Product (live, with links) and moat.
4. Market (sourced) and wedge.
5. Traction — real weekly metrics; if none, the 90-day plan and what will be measured.
6. Business model and unit economics (per print order, per repair, per node).
7. Go-to-market and growth loops (class reps, rewards, ambassadors, nodes).
8. Competition (honest table).
9. Team and advisors.
10. Financial plan (projections summary) and use of funds with milestones.
11. Risks and mitigations.
12. Cap table and terms (current and proposed), exit thinking.

## Legal & IP readiness report

1. Current status table: item · status (done / pending / not needed yet) · owner · cost · source.
   Items: entity, PAN/TAN, bank, GST, Shops & Establishments, trade licence, Udyam, DPIIT, KSUM ID,
   professional tax, EPF/ESI, trademark(s), copyright, domain ownership, privacy policy & terms,
   e-commerce rule compliance, founders' agreement, employment/intern agreements with IP assignment,
   node/franchise agreement template, repair job-card terms, e-waste handling, copyright policy.
2. Gaps ranked by risk and deadline.
3. 30/60/90-day compliance plan with estimated costs (quote ranges only with sources).
4. IP plan: marks and classes to file, owner entity, budget, timeline.

## Closing sections (every report)

- **Inputs still needed** — every placeholder, who can provide it.
- **Assumptions** — numbered, with the reason for each.
- **Sources** — title, publisher, URL, date checked.
- **Professional sign-off required** — what a CA / advocate / CS / TM-patent attorney must verify or
  sign, and why.
- **Next steps** — who does what, by when.
