# Investor finder

Goal: a short, verified list of investors who actually back companies like T FIX at its current
stage — not a long list of famous names. Wrong-fit outreach wastes the founders' credibility.

## 1. Define the fit profile first (write it at the top of the output)
- **Stage**: idea / pre-seed / seed / Series A / growth.
- **Ticket**: amount sought now (e.g. ₹25 lakh–₹1 crore pre-seed).
- **Sector tags**: campus/student services, consumer services, device repair & recommerce (circular
  economy), print-on-demand, SaaS for small shops (T Print / node software), edtech-adjacent
  (Academy), climate/impact (paper saved, e-waste).
- **Geography**: Kerala / South India / India / international with India mandate.
- **Instrument**: grant, convertible (iSAFE/CCD), equity, revenue-based finance, debt.

## 2. Search (live, every time)
Use web search with several angles; prefer the investor's own site, Inc42/Entrackr/YourStory/ET deal
news, Tracxn/Crunchbase pages, and government lists. Useful queries:
- "<sector> startup pre-seed India <year> raised led by"
- "repair recommerce startup India funding <year>"
- "campus student services startup India seed round"
- "Kerala startup angel investment <year>"
- "<fund name> thesis consumer services India portfolio"
- International: "India-focused venture fund consumer services seed", "circular economy fund India",
  "impact investor student livelihoods India".

## 3. Verify each candidate (keep only if all are true)
- Has made at least one relevant investment in the last ~18–24 months (name the deal + source).
- Invests at the needed stage and ticket (from their site or a deal report).
- Invests in India / Kerala (check mandate; many international funds only do Series A+).
- Accepts inbound or has a clear route (form, accelerator, warm intro, event).

## 4. Output table
| Rank | Investor | Type | Stage & ticket | Why fit (thesis + similar deal, with date) | Route to approach | Source(s) |

Then: top 5 to approach first and why; what to send (deck, one-pager, metrics); red flags (fees
charged to startups for "access" → avoid).
